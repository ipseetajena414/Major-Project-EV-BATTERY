## 1. Role & Scope of Member 3 in the Multi-Agent System

In this major project, three autonomous agents / members collaborate to predict EV battery degradation and real-world driving range:
- **Member 1 (Data Engineering & Curation):** Ingests raw cycling datasets (NMC, LFP, Sodium-ion, Solid-State), cleans anomalies, calculates cell health metrics, and builds the validated `master_table.parquet`.
- **Member 2 (Machine Learning & Degradation Forecasting):** Trains LSTM/Bi-LSTM models on `master_table.parquet` for SOH, capacity and RUL. Its served inference API (`Member2Predictor`) exposes LightGBM models; the Bi-LSTM checkpoint is delivered but not served.
- **Member 3 (Vehicle Dynamics & Range Calculator — THIS ROLE):**
  - Implements a lumped-parameter vehicle dynamics physics engine.
  - Ingests drive cycles (time, speed, road grade) and vehicle specifications (mass, drag coefficient, frontal area, rolling resistance, battery capacity).
  - Integrates electrical traction and regenerative braking power.
  - Models temperature-dependent air density and HVAC thermal loads.
  - Takes Member 2's predicted SOH trajectories and maps cell degradation to driving-range decay. Project documents use `650 km → 550 km` as an **illustrative narrative**, not a validated result.

### B. Major Technical Audit & Engineering Overhaul (Recent Milestone)
A rigorous technical audit was conducted, resolving critical physical and numerical deficiencies:

1. **Interval-Based Mechanical Work Integration (Fixing Decoupled Kinetic Energy):**
   - *Previous Defect:* The starter code computed nodal acceleration using 3-point central differences ($a_i = \frac{v_{i+1}-v_{i-1}}{t_{i+1}-t_{i-1}}$) and multiplied by node velocity. On triangular speed profiles ($0 \to 10 \to 0\text{ m/s}$), peak velocity had $a=0$ while endpoints had $v=0$, causing kinetic energy of acceleration to be completely missed ($0.0\text{ kWh}$ integrated).
   - *Resolution:* Re-architected to **interval-based energy integration**:
     $$\Delta t = t_{i+1} - t_i, \quad \bar{v} = \frac{v_i + v_{i+1}}{2}, \quad \Delta s = \bar{v} \Delta t$$
     $$W_{\text{inertia}} = \frac{1}{2} m_{\text{eff}} (v_{i+1}^2 - v_i^2) \quad \text{(Exact analytical kinetic energy integral)}$$
     $$\bar{F}_{\text{aero}} = \frac{1}{2} \rho C_d A \left(\frac{v_i^2 + v_{i+1}^2}{2}\right), \quad W_{\text{aero}} = \bar{F}_{\text{aero}} \Delta s$$
     $$W_{\text{roll}} = m g C_{rr} \cos\theta \Delta s \quad (\bar{v} > 0), \quad W_{\text{grade}} = m g \sin\theta \Delta s$$
     $$W_{\text{wheel}} = W_{\text{inertia}} + W_{\text{aero}} + W_{\text{roll}} + W_{\text{grade}}$$
   - Guarantees exact conservation of energy ($\oint m a v dt = 0$) on closed drive cycles.

2. **Rotational Inertia Equivalent Mass ($\delta = 1.05$):**
   - Replaced point-mass assumption ($F = m \cdot a$) with effective rotational inertia:
     $$m_{\text{eff}} = m_{\text{vehicle}} \cdot (1 + \delta), \quad \delta = 0.05 \text{ (5% rotational inertia of wheels, rotors, motor)}$$

3. **Air Density from Temperature, Station Pressure or Altitude (extended in Phase 2):**
   - Eliminated the static sea-level assumption ($1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$). Density is always the ideal-gas law for dry air:
     $$\rho=\frac{p}{R_{\text{spec}}\,T_{\text{K}}},\qquad R_{\text{spec}}=287.058\ \text{J/(kg·K)},\qquad T_{\text{K}}=T_{\text{amb}}+273.15$$
     which captures the $+9.5\%$ higher aerodynamic drag in winter ($-10^\circ\text{C}$, $\rho = 1.341\text{ kg/m}^3$) versus mild weather ($+15^\circ\text{C}$, $\rho = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$).
   - Phase 2 adds the two missing physical inputs. Pressure comes from whichever of these is supplied, in this precedence (so altitude is never applied twice):
     `pressure_pa` (a measured station pressure) $\succ$ ISA troposphere at `altitude_m` $\succ$ standard sea level $101{,}325\ \text{Pa}$.
     $$p(h)=p_0\Bigl(1-\frac{L\,h}{T_0}\Bigr)^{g_0/(R_{\text{spec}}L)},\qquad L=0.0065\ \text{K/m},\ T_0=288.15\ \text{K},\ \tfrac{g_0}{R_{\text{spec}}L}=5.25588$$
     documented as valid for $-500\ \text{m}\le h\le 11{,}000\ \text{m}$ (the tropopause); outside that range the input is rejected rather than extrapolated.
   - Temperature follows the same rule in reverse: a supplied `ambient_c` is used **exactly as measured and receives no lapse correction**, because a ground observation already is the ground temperature. The ISA temperature $T(h)=T_0-Lh$ is used only when `ambient_c` is omitted.
   - When all three environmental arguments are omitted, the configured `air_density_kg_m3` is used unchanged — the legacy behaviour, asserted per shipped profile in tests.
   - `estimate()` now reports `atmospheric_pressure_pa`, `altitude_m`, `air_density_source` (`CONFIG_REFERENCE` or `IDEAL_GAS_LAW`) and `headwind_mps` additively; every new parameter is optional.

4. **Empirical HVAC Thermal Comfort Model:**
   - Converted `ambient_c` from an unused pass-through argument into an active thermal demand model:
     - **Comfort Deadband ($18^\circ\text{C} \le T_{\text{amb}} \le 22^\circ\text{C}$):** Base ventilation only ($P_{\text{default}}$, $0.25\text{ kW}$ default).
     - **Heating ($T_{\text{amb}} < 18^\circ\text{C}$):** $P_{\text{heat}} = \min(P_{\text{cap,heat}}, P_{\text{default}} + 0.18 \times (18.0 - T_{\text{amb}}))\text{ kW}$, where $P_{\text{cap,heat}} = \min(15.0, \max(6.0, P_{\text{default}}))$.
     - **Cooling ($T_{\text{amb}} > 22^\circ\text{C}$):** $P_{\text{cool}} = \min(P_{\text{cap,cool}}, P_{\text{default}} + 0.12 \times (T_{\text{amb}} - 22.0))\text{ kW}$, where $P_{\text{cap,cool}} = \min(15.0, \max(3.5, P_{\text{default}}))$.
     - Strictly continuous and non-decreasing departing the comfort deadband, bounded by $15.0\text{ kW}$, and overridable via `--hvac-kw`.

5. **Regenerative Braking from the Instantaneous Wheel-Power Profile:**
   - Real electric motors collapse back-EMF near zero speed, so availability is a function of speed ($\beta(v) = 0$ for $v \le 0.5\text{ m/s}$, linear ramp to $\beta=1.0$ at $v \ge 2.0\text{ m/s}$). Two earlier generations of this code decided blending and traction-vs-braking once per interval: the committed baseline evaluated $\beta$ at the interval-average speed, and the uncommitted Phase-0 revision weighted $\beta$ by kinetic energy and applied it to the interval's **net** wheel work:
     $$\beta_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\int_{v_0}^{v_1} \beta(v)\,v\,dv}{\tfrac{1}{2}(v_1^2 - v_0^2)}$$
     Neither is valid once braking work also comes from aerodynamic, rolling and grade forces. Measured on the shipped default vehicle: the kinetic weighting credits $\beta_{\text{eff}}=0.600$ on a 2.0→0.5 m/s ramp where the power-weighted value is 0.589 ($+1.9\%$ over-recovery); and on a 40→10 m/s interval with heavy aerodynamic load the wheel power is traction above $\approx31\text{ m/s}$ and braking below it, yet the single-sign rule reported **zero traction** for $\approx96\text{ kJ}$ of positive wheel work while still crediting regeneration.
   - Phase 1 replaces it with an event-splitting integrator. Per interval, with $v(t)$ piecewise linear and $\theta$ piecewise constant (interval grade average),
     $$P_{\text{wheel}}(v) = \big(m_{\text{eff}} a + m g (C_{rr}\cos\theta + \sin\theta)\big)v + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho C_d A\,v^{3},\qquad P_{\text{brake}}(t)=\max(0,-P_{\text{wheel}})$$
     $$P_{\text{regen}}(t)=\min\big(\eta_{\text{regen}}\,\beta(v(t))\,P_{\text{brake}}(t),\;P_{\max}\big)$$
     Break points are inserted at the two blend thresholds, at the sign change $v^{\ast}=\sqrt{-(m_{\text{eff}}a+F_{\text{resist}})/(\tfrac{1}{2}\rho C_d A)}$, and at every crossing of $P_{\max}$ (found from the closed-form critical points of the power quartic, then 80-step bisection). Inside each sub-piece $\beta$ is affine in $v$, so all terms are integrated exactly from the velocity moments $\int v^{n}dt$, $n=1..4$.
   - `max_regen_power_kw` is the **battery electrical charge-power limit**, applied to instantaneous power (a capped sub-piece contributes $P_{\max}\Delta t$), not to an interval average.
   - Complete braking ledger, each term reported: `mechanical_braking_kwh` $=\int P_{\text{brake}}dt$, `regen_mechanical_absorbed_kwh` (mechanical share routed through the motor), `regen_recovered_kwh` $=\eta_{\text{regen}}\times$ that, and `friction_braking_kwh` = mechanical braking minus the share the motor took (blend- or cap-limited). The efficiency loss itself is electrical conversion loss and is not double-booked as friction. Ledger identities are asserted in tests, so energy is neither created, lost, nor counted twice.
   - Constant-speed descent braking is captured: grade resistance alone can hold $P_{\text{wheel}}<0$ with $\Delta KE = 0$.
   - Convergence, as measured rather than claimed: refining the *same* trajectory (2×, 4×, 8× linear interpolation inside every interval of the 1 Hz UDDS trace) leaves every ledger term unchanged to $\sim10^{-13}$ relative, because the integrator is exact for the given polyline. Decimating that trace to 2 Hz / 5 Hz changes net cycle energy by −0.97 % / −3.48 %; those are **input-representation** differences (a coarser description of the road), not solver error, and the decimated polyline is itself integrated exactly.
   - `NET_GENERATING_DESCENT` (infinite range) is returned without crashing when net cycle energy is non-positive, and the status is now identical for every subdivision of the same motion. A result limitation states explicitly that part of that recovery is the vehicle's own initial kinetic energy, harvested once, so the value is not a sustainable repeated-cycle consumption rate and must never be reported as a driving range.
   - `effective_regen_blend_factor()` remains in the module as a legacy diagnostic only; it is not used by `estimate()`.

6. **Steady Along-Track Relative Wind (Phase 2):**
   - The aerodynamic term was rewritten in terms of relative air speed, with an explicit sign convention: `headwind_mps` is the longitudinal wind component, **positive = headwind** (air moving toward the vehicle nose), negative = tailwind.
     $$u=v+w,\qquad F_{\text{aero}}=\tfrac12\rho C_d A\,u|u|,\qquad P_{\text{aero}}=F_{\text{aero}}\,v$$
     Force uses relative air speed; power uses **ground** speed, which is what actually does work along the road. A headwind therefore raises drag and a tailwind lowers it, and when a tailwind exceeds the vehicle speed ($u<0$) the air pushes the car — aero becomes thrust.
   - Numerically this stays exact rather than approximate. Within one side of $u=0$ the wheel power is a cubic in $v$
     $$P_{\text wheel}(v)=L v+Q v^{2}+C v^{3},\qquad L=L_0+k w^{2},\ Q=2k w,\ C=k,\ k=\tfrac12\rho C_d A,$$
     so $\beta(v)P_{\text{wheel}}(v)$ is still a quartic and the Phase 1 machinery (closed-form critical points, bisection on the charge-power crossings, velocity moments $n=1..4$) applies unchanged; the integrator adds one extra split at $u=0$. With zero wind the code executes the identical statements in the identical operand order, which is why the Phase 1 baseline numbers are bit-reproducible.
   - A crosswind is **rejected**, not approximated: `crosswind_mps` raises `ValueError` for any non-zero value. This model is one-dimensional and $C_dA$ under yaw is a different, unmodelled problem; silently folding a crosswind into the longitudinal term would be an invented number.
   - Validated against an independent midpoint oracle of the documented continuous model (wind- and grade-aware, written from the equations and never from the engine) to $\sim10^{-12}$ relative on coast-down and mixed grade+wind intervals; the committed tests use 5e-4 tolerance.
   - Assumptions stated in the result limitations when wind is supplied: steady, uniform, no gusts, no boundary-layer shear, no altitude-dependent wind profile, altitude never applied twice.

### E. Advanced HVAC and Cabin Thermal Modelling (Phase 3)

Phase 3 adds an opt-in, physically structured HVAC model next to the existing empirical comfort curve. Nothing was replaced: `hvac_model_mode` defaults to `EMPIRICAL`, the legacy whole-cycle comfort curve still runs its original statements, and none of the ten shipped `vehicle_configs/` profiles carries a cabin parameter, so an existing call, profile or published output is untouched unless the thermal model is explicitly requested.

**Modes and actuators.** One actuator per mode, deliberately, so heating paths can never both claim the same kilowatt-hour and no unstated blend logic decides what runs:

| Mode | Actuator | Conversion | Output label |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `EMPIRICAL` (default) | — | empirical comfort curve, unchanged | `CONFIG_DEFAULT` / `COMFORT_DEADBAND_VENTILATION` / `EMPIRICAL_HEATING_DEMAND` / `EMPIRICAL_COOLING_DEMAND` |
| `HEAT_PUMP` | heating only | $P_{\text{elec}} = Q_{\text{heat}} / COP_{\text{heat}}$ | `CABIN_HEAT_PUMP` |
| `PTC` | heating only | $P_{\text{elec}} = Q_{\text{heat}} / \eta_{\text{PTC}}$, $\eta_{\text{PTC}}\in(0,1]$ | `CABIN_PTC` |
| `AC_COOLING` | cooling only | $P_{\text{elec}} = \lvert Q_{\text{cool}}\rvert / COP_{\text{cool}}$ | `CABIN_AC_COOLING` |

`HYBRID` and `AUTO` are **not** implemented: the Phase 3 specification forbids combining heating methods without a separately documented and tested hybrid mode, and an automatic selection would be an unstated tuning decision. A demand the selected mode cannot serve leaves the actuator off and raises `cabin_setpoint_unmet` in the output plus a `limitations` note, instead of silently serving it.

**Plant, control and integration.** A single cabin node, one interval at a time:

$$C_{\text{cabin}}\frac{\mathrm{d}T_{\text{cab}}}{\mathrm{d}t}=UA\,(T_{\text{amb}}-T_{\text{cab}})+Q_{\text{solar}}+Q_{\text{occ}}+Q_{\text{HVAC}}$$

with $Q_{\text{HVAC}}>0$ heating, $Q_{\text{HVAC}}<0$ cooling, and the drawn electrical power always non-negative. The control request is the steady-state load to the setpoint plus a proportional term, held constant across the interval (zero-order hold):

$$Q_{\text{demand}}=UA\,(T_{\text{set}}-T_{\text{amb}})-Q_{\text{solar}}-Q_{\text{occ}}+K_p\,(T_{\text{set}}-T_{\text{cab}}),\qquad K_p=C_{\text{cabin}}/\tau_{\text{recovery}}$$

The ODE is solved in closed form per interval, $T_{n+1}=T_\infty+(T_n-T_\infty)e^{-UA\,\Delta t/C_{\text{cabin}}}$, so pure drift is exact at any step size and $\Delta t$ only enters through the held $Q_{\text{HVAC}}$. `cabin_ua_w_per_k = 0` is rejected for a cabin mode (no conductive leak means no steady state, and held heat would march linearly past the setpoint). The discrete error map is $e_{n+1}=e_n[(1+k)\rho-k]$ with $k=K_p/UA$, $\rho=e^{-UA\Delta t/C}$; requiring $k\le1$, i.e. $\tau_{\text{recovery}}\ge C/UA$, makes $|{(1+k)\rho-k|}<1$ for **every** allowed interval, and `VehicleConfig.validate()` enforces that bound instead of letting a scenario oscillate.

**Energy accounting.** `auxiliary_energy_kwh` and `hvac_energy_kwh` are now separate accumulators, `aux_hvac_energy_kwh` keeps its historical meaning as their exact sum, and $E_{\text{cycle}} = E_{\text{traction}} - E_{\text{regen}} + E_{\text{aux}} + E_{\text{HVAC}}$. HVAC never sits in the auxiliary bucket. Inside the HVAC bucket the blower base `hvac_power_kw_default` is counted exactly once: a cabin mode bills `base_kw + actuator` (with no actuator demand it bills the blower alone), `EMPIRICAL` takes its entire HVAC number from the comfort curve whose floor is that same base, and a manual `hvac_power_kw` replaces the base instead of adding to it, which is the pre-Phase-3 meaning of the argument. `auxiliary_energy_kwh` is `auxiliary_power_kw × duration` in every mode, with no blower in it. Cabin modes additionally report the *thermal* ledger (`hvac_heating_thermal_energy_kwh`, `hvac_cooling_thermal_energy_kwh`) next to the electrical one, so a COP is never confused with an efficiency; fourteen additive result keys appear, all `null` in `EMPIRICAL` mode.

**Caps stay on the electrical side, and thermal follows.** `max_heating_electrical_kw` / `max_cooling_electrical_kw` bound the actuator, and blower base plus actuator never exceeds the engine's 15 kW HVAC electrical ceiling — the actuator is throttled to the remaining headroom and the delivered heat recomputed from the throttled input, so the two ledgers can never disagree. When a cap binds, `hvac_power_limited` is true and the cabin approaches but does not hold the setpoint. `hvac_power_kw` (`--hvac-kw`) keeps its established precedence as an **electrical** override that short-circuits every mode; the output then records the requested `hvac_model_mode` plus a `limitations` note stating that the model did not run.

**Provenance.** Every cabin field is optional, validated against a documented envelope, and labelled illustrative. The defaults are compact-EV engineering placeholders ($C=10^6$ J/K, $UA=150$ W/K, setpoint 22 °C); the example profile in `examples/hvac_cabin_illustrative_config.json` uses $C=250\,000$ J/K with $UA=250$ W/K (1000 s plant constant) so a cabin visibly drifts over one cycle. The COP curves are clipped linear approximations anchored at the **EN 14511 A7 (7 °C outdoor air) heating and A35 (35 °C outdoor air) cooling test duties** — the air-to-water spelling A7/W35 adds a 35 °C water supply temperature that does not apply to an air-delivered cabin — and EN 14511 prescribes those *conditions*, not any COP, so the anchor values (3.0 heating, 3.5 cooling), their 0.05/K slopes and their clip bands are illustrative engineering assumptions rather than measured vehicle-specific data or standard-prescribed figures. No measured vehicle-specific COP map was available, so none is claimed. This is explicitly **not** a fully calibrated vehicle cabin simulation: no glazing/ventilation split, no humidity or latent load, no defrost, no seat heaters, no battery thermal management, no pre-conditioning schedule, no sun angle.

**Measured behaviour, on the example profile, UDDS (1369 s), −10 °C, 100 % SOH, ambient-soaked cabin:**

| Mode | HVAC electrical | Heat delivered | Cabin end | Estimated range |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `EMPIRICAL` | 2.012 kWh | not modelled | not modelled | 212.8 km |
| `HEAT_PUMP` | 1.932 kWh | 3.949 kWh (COP 2.15) | 19.93 °C (setpoint held) | 217.4 km |
| `PTC` | 3.137 kWh | 2.890 kWh (η 0.95) | 13.71 °C (capped response) | 164.2 km |
| `AC_COOLING` | 0.095 kWh (blower only) | 0.0 kWh | −8.96 °C (unmet; gains only) | 429.0 km |

The table is the point of Phase 3: at the same ambient, the *actuator technology* now changes the range (217.4 km heat-pump vs 164.2 km resistive), a mode with no compatible actuator costs only the blower and reports the setpoint as unmet rather than inventing heat, and the thermal and electrical ledgers stay tied by the configured COP or efficiency (3.949 / 1.8369 actuator-only = 2.15; 2.890 / 3.0419 = 0.95).

**Convergence, measured not asserted** (illustrative cabin: $C=10^5$ J/K, $UA=10^3$ W/K, 600 s at 15 °C ambient from a 5 °C soak): HVAC electrical energy over the cycle is 0.277595 / 0.268619 / 0.264420 / 0.262394 kWh at 10 / 20 / 40 / 80 intervals, with successive differences 0.008976 → 0.004199 → 0.002026 kWh, i.e. ratios 0.4678 and 0.4826 — first-order ($O(\Delta t)$) convergence toward the continuous-time solution, whose Richardson estimate is 0.260368 kWh. Contraction matches the derived factor: measured per-step error ratios equal $(1+k)\rho-k$ to 9 decimal places, and with the configured $k=1$ the factor is $+0.098$ at $\Delta t=60$ s (monotone approach) but $-0.729$ at $\Delta t=200$ s and $-0.995$ at $\Delta t=600$ s (bounded ringing — still contracting, per the $|factor|<1$ bound, but no longer monotone). The pure-drift case is grid-independent by construction: with no compatible actuator every refinement from 5 to 120 intervals returns $30+15e^{-6}$ °C to 12 decimal places.

### F. Scenario Experiment Framework (Phase 4)

Phase 4 automates what the three previous phases built. It adds no new physics: `scenario_engine.py` and `run_experiments.py` validate drive cycles, generate deterministic scenarios, sweep a configurable matrix through the *existing* `RangeCalculator.estimate()` API, and write provenance-complete results plus figures. No competing simulator exists, no result dictionary changed meaning, and nothing under `member1_pipeline/`, `member2_modeling/`, `DATA/processed/` or `integration/outputs/` is touched.

**Drive-cycle validation and provenance (§3).** `inspect_cycle()` audits a trace into a 21-field `CycleAudit` record — row count, columns, duration, trapezoidal distance, sampling interval (uniform/min/max/mode), mean and max speed, idle fraction, grade span, SHA-256 of the CSV and of its `drive_cycles/source/` file, claimed identity, warnings. `load_cycle_rows()` refuses anything the engine would otherwise average over: non-increasing time, negative or non-numeric or non-finite speed, a missing cell, a speed above 50 m/s (a mph/kph unit error, not a car), a gap over 600 s, a duration outside 10–7200 s, zero distance, and a bad header. Each shipped trace carries its verified label:

| Cycle | Duration | Distance | Provenance label |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `udds_city.csv` | 1369 s | 11.990 km | `UNVERIFIED_TRANSCRIBED_CYCLE` |
| `hwfet_highway.csv` | 765 s | 16.507 km | `UNVERIFIED_TRANSCRIBED_CYCLE` |
| `example_cycle.csv` | 60 s | 0.270 km | `ILLUSTRATIVE_SYNTHETIC_CYCLE` |

The two laboratory traces match the published UDDS/HWFET totals, and the audit says so in exactly those words: agreement of duration and distance is *corroboration, not certification*, because no publisher checksum or download record lives in this repository. The HWFET file is named `HWFET.txt` but its internal header reads `HWYCOL.TXT Highway Fuel Economy Test`; that discrepancy is reported, not resolved, and is the reason the label stays unverified. `example_cycle.csv` is never presented as a standard cycle and no WLTP data was fabricated: `DRIVE_CYCLES` lists only the three files that exist. All of it is generated rather than hand-written — `python tools/audit_drive_cycles.py --out DRIVE_CYCLE_PROVENANCE_REPORT.md` rebuilds [DRIVE_CYCLE_PROVENANCE_REPORT.md](DRIVE_CYCLE_PROVENANCE_REPORT.md) from live audits, and a test regenerates it and asserts every line but the timestamp is byte-identical.

**Scenario generation (§4).** Six scenarios, built by `piecewise_profile(segments, dt_s)` from `(phase, duration, v_start, v_end, grade_pct)` tuples with shared boundaries — no RNG, so a rebuild is byte-identical. A and B drive the *transcribed traces unchanged* (`TRANSCRIBED_CYCLE_SCENARIO`); C is the HWFET trace pinned to −10 °C, D is the UDDS trace pinned to 38 °C with a 600 W solar gain; E and F are `SYNTHETIC_SCENARIO`. E is a mountain pass: 7 phases with a sustained +6 % climb and a sustained −6 % descent, and its documented grade note states plainly that the climb and the descent do not cancel exactly after drivetrain and regen losses. F is an aggressive profile whose 9 phases include a final 20 s idle. Every synthetic trace passes the same gates as a loaded file (max 6.0 m/s² acceleration, max 50 m/s speed, 10–7200 s duration, ≤600 s sampling), and the scenario records their assumptions and the "Not an EPA or WLTP cycle" disclaimer into the manifest.

**Runner and output schema (§5–§7).** `run_experiments.py` resolves exactly one matrix source — `--preset`, `--matrix <json>` or `--single` — and sweeps scenario × vehicle × ambient × SOH × HVAC mode × headwind × altitude. Five presets ship: `main` (28 runs), `hvac` (24), `soh` (12), `thermal` (4), `attribution` (3); `examples/matrix_example.json` is the copyable definition. Ambient sweeps are *pinned* where a scenario is defined by a temperature, and the runner says so out loud instead of silently collapsing the axis. Each run writes into a **new** directory under `experiments/results/<experiment_id>/`; an existing directory is refused (exit 2), so history is never overwritten. `scenario_results.csv` has 56 columns, each declared in `RESULT_COLUMNS` with its unit and its source (engine field, run input, or probe), and the manifest records them as `units_and_columns` plus experiment date, git identity, per-profile and per-cycle hashes, scenario parameters, HVAC settings, 8 model assumptions, the boundary statement, and the reproducibility block described below. Exit status: 0 when all runs succeeded, 1 when any row is `FAILED` (a failed row keeps its inputs and all 56 columns — failures are reported, not dropped), 2 for a usage error or a refused overwrite.

**Reproducibility block.** `SOURCE_FILES` names every file that can move a published number — `range_calculator.py`, `scenario_engine.py`, `run_experiments.py`, `tools/experiment_plots.py`, `tools/audit_drive_cycles.py` — and `software_version` records a `sha256` and a `member3/…_path` for each, alongside `python`, `implementation`, `platform` and whether matplotlib was importable (the SVG fallback is recorded as `NOT_INSTALLED (SVG fallback used)`, which is what these experiments used). A file passed to `--cabin-config` is hashed into `hvac_settings.cabin_config_files` because its bytes set the thermal result; the built-in illustrative cabin set is instead recorded by value in `hvac_settings.illustrative_cabin_set`. `code_version` reports commit, branch and `working_tree_dirty`, and its `note` states that the commit is the last *committed* state and, while the tree is dirty, does not represent the uncommitted source that produced these results — the per-file hashes do. The verification rerun confirms the property this is meant to guarantee: the same matrix on the same bytes reproduces every numeric column exactly (54 compared columns × 4 rows, 0 differences), with only timestamps, the experiment id and the manifest's own checksums differing.

**Figures (§8).** `tools/experiment_plots.py` renders seven report figures — range across scenarios, energy consumption, temperature impact, HVAC contribution, traction versus regeneration (stacked, with the regen credit drawn *below* the baseline), SOH versus range, and the mountain phase ledger. Each carries a title, subtitle, axis labels with units, legend, the scenario/configuration identification and a footer naming the illustrative assumptions and the `SYNTHETIC_SCENARIO` rows. Matplotlib is used when importable (PNG); otherwise the identical `Chart` specifications render as deterministic stdlib SVG, which is what this interpreter produced (`matplotlib_available: false` in every `figure_manifest.json`). One disclosed reference configuration per chart keeps every comparison like-for-like.

- **Remaining risks:** both Member 2 models underperform a persistence baseline; Bi-LSTM is not served by the API; no uncertainty bounds; vehicle dynamics uncalibrated; prediction horizon is positional (one observed record), not a physical cycle horizon; cell-to-pack mapping is linear.
- **Action Required:**
  - Obtain LFP predictions (Member 2 LFP models use a 29-feature schema without `temp_*`) and run the adapter; Na-ion has no supported model.
  - Extend the adapter only after Member 2 provides pack-level or multi-cell output; do not average cell SOH into pack energy without a documented aggregation model.
  - Re-run the pipeline when a vehicle profile is validated, and compare against certified cycles once those are attributed.

### Task 4: Cell-to-Pack Aggregation & Internal Resistance Expansion
- **Current State:** Usable energy scales linearly ($E = E_{\text{nom}} \times \text{fraction} \times SOH/100$).
- **Action Required:**
  - Account for series-string weakest-cell limitation in high-voltage EV packs.
  - Implement internal resistance ($R_0$) rise: As the battery ages, $R_0$ increases ($I^2 R$ heat losses increase), causing terminal voltage to hit cutoff earlier under aggressive acceleration.

### Task 5: Automated Scenario Runner & Visualization Scripts — Implemented (Phase 4)
- **Current state:** `run_experiments.py` plus `scenario_engine.py` and `tools/experiment_plots.py` deliver the ambient sweep ($-10$ / $+20$ / $+38$ °C), the drive-style sweep (urban UDDS-transcription, highway HWFET-transcription, and the synthetic mountain/aggressive profiles), the HVAC-mode and SOH sweeps, CSV export with a provenance manifest, and seven report figures. See Section 2F.
- **Not done, deliberately:** the *chemistry* axis (NMC vs LFP vs Na-ion) is **not** swept. The range engine has no chemistry-specific physics — SOH enters only as a linear multiplier on usable energy — so a chemistry comparison would be a meaningless label over identical numbers. The Phase 4 brief also excludes internal-resistance ageing from this phase.
- **Not done:** WLTP remains absent (no fabrication, per §3 of the Phase 4 brief); profiles remain uncalibrated, so the scenario comparison uses one explicitly designated reference configuration instead of cross-vehicle ranking.
- **Remaining action:** promote the transcribed cycles to attributed official cycles, then re-run the presets and benchmark against certified figures.
